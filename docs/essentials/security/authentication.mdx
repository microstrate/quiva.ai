Protect your QuivaWorks account with multi-factor authentication (MFA) and passkeys.

## Why Enable MFA?

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Stronger sign-in" icon="shield-check">
    A stolen password on its own is no longer enough to reach your account
  </Card>

  <Card title="Phishing resistance" icon="fish">
    Passkeys are bound to the site that issued them and can't be replayed elsewhere
  </Card>

  <Card title="Account recovery" icon="life-ring">
    Enabling MFA issues recovery codes you can use if you lose your device
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
QuivaWorks prompts you to set up MFA each time you log in until it's enabled, and the setup screen offers **Set Up Later**. MFA is never mandatory for any role — but we strongly recommend enabling it.
</Note>

## Setting Up Multi-Factor Authentication

Both methods live in the same place: open **Settings** from your user menu, then the **Password and authentication** tab.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Passkey (Recommended)">
    Passkeys let you sign in with biometrics or a device PIN instead of a password.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the tab">
        **Settings → Password and authentication**
      </Step>

      <Step title="Add a passkey">
        In the **Passkey** section, click **Add**. The screen is titled "Add passkey".
      </Step>

      <Step title="Complete setup">
        Follow your device's prompts — Touch ID, Face ID, Windows Hello, a device password, a PIN, or a hardware security key.
      </Step>

      <Step title="Save your recovery codes">
        Store them somewhere safe before you close the screen
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Info>
    Existing passkeys are listed under **Manage**, where you can remove one or remove them all.
    </Info>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Authenticator App">
    Use time-based one-time passwords (TOTP) with an app such as Google Authenticator, Authy, Microsoft Authenticator, or a password manager that supports TOTP.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the tab">
        **Settings → Password and authentication**
      </Step>

      <Step title="Add an authenticator app">
        Under **Two-factor authentication → Authenticator App**, click **Add**. The screen is titled "Add authenticator app".
      </Step>

      <Step title="Scan the QR code">
        Open your authenticator app and scan the code displayed
      </Step>

      <Step title="Enter the verification code">
        Type the 6-digit code from your app to confirm setup
      </Step>

      <Step title="Save your recovery codes">
        Store them securely
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Recovery Codes

Recovery codes give you a way back in if you lose your MFA device.

<Warning>
Store recovery codes in a password manager, an encrypted file, or a physical safe. Never leave them in email, a shared document, or unencrypted notes.
</Warning>

### How they work

- You receive **10 codes**
- Each code can be used **once**
- Issuing a new set invalidates the old one

### Viewing your recovery codes

1. Open **Settings → Password and authentication**
2. Scroll to the **Recovery codes** section
3. Click **View** to see the current set, or **Get New** to issue a fresh one

An email notification is sent whenever recovery codes are viewed.

### Using a recovery code

On the two-factor challenge screen, switch to the **Recovery Code** method tab, enter one of your codes, and continue. Issue a new set afterwards if you're running low.

<Note>
If you've used all your codes, a root or admin user can issue a new set for you from **Issue new recovery codes** in the Users dot menu.
</Note>

## Password Management

### Password requirements

Your password must be:

- At least **8 characters** and at most **72 characters**
- Containing at least one **uppercase** letter
- Containing at least one **lowercase** letter
- Containing at least one **number**
- Containing at least one **special character**

Common words are rejected.

<Tip>
Use a password manager to generate and store a long, unique password.
</Tip>

### Changing your password

1. Open **Settings → Password and authentication**
2. In the **Password** section, click **Change password**
3. Confirm your identity — changing a password requires passing two-factor authentication first
4. Enter and confirm your new password

<Info>
All your other sessions are terminated; your current session stays signed in. You'll receive an email confirming the change.
</Info>

### Forgot your password?

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start the reset">
    On the login screen, click **Forgot Password**, enter the email address associated with your account, and click **Reset Password**. Only the email address is needed.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Follow the link in your email">
    You'll receive an email containing a password reset link.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set a new password">
    Open the link, enter a new password meeting the requirements above, and confirm it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Reset links are valid for **2 days**. Request a new one if yours has expired.
</Warning>

<Info>
Resetting your password from the emailed link while signed out terminates **every** session on the account — including any an attacker is holding. A confirmation email is sent either way.
</Info>

## Best Practices

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Use a unique password">
    Never reuse a password across services. A password manager makes this practical.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Enable MFA now, not later">
    Set it up when you create your account rather than after an incident.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Store recovery codes securely">
    A password manager or a physical safe. Not email, not shared notes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Prefer passkeys">
    Passkeys can't be phished or replayed, and they're faster to use than a one-time code.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Register a second passkey">
    A backup passkey on another device saves you from falling back to recovery codes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Review your sessions">
    Check [active sessions](/essentials/security/sessions) periodically and terminate anything you don't recognise.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Lost access to your MFA device">
    1. Use a recovery code to log in
    2. Set up a new MFA method immediately
    3. Issue a new set of recovery codes
    4. If you've lost your recovery codes too, ask a root or admin user to issue a new set from the Users dot menu
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Authenticator app codes rejected">
    - Check your device's clock is synchronised — TOTP depends on accurate time
    - Use the current code; they refresh every 30 seconds
    - Remove and re-add the account in your authenticator app
    - Use a recovery code if the problem persists
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Passkey not recognised">
    - Confirm your device and browser support passkeys
    - Register a backup passkey on another device
    - Fall back to your authenticator app or a recovery code
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Didn't receive the password reset email">
    - Check your spam or junk folder
    - Confirm you entered the correct email address — the reset form matches on email alone
    - Allow a few minutes for delivery, then request another reset
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
